THE MACHINE THAT CHANGED HISTORY

WHAT WAS EAGLE?

Eagle was the Lunar Module (LM) used during NASA's Apollo 11 mission. It was designed to transport astronauts from lunar orbit down to the Moon's surface and then return them to the Command and Service Module in lunar orbit. Unlike ordinary aircraft, Eagle had to work in an environment without an atmosphere, where conventional wings and aerodynamic flight were impossible. Its landing and ascent depended on rocket propulsion and precise guidance systems.

ENGINEERING / SPACECRAFT DESIGN

TWO STAGES. ONE MISSION.

The Lunar Module consisted of two major sections. Each had a different purpose, and both were essential to the success of the landing.

01. DESCENT STAGE

The lower section of Eagle was responsible for bringing the spacecraft safely down to the lunar surface. Its descent engine controlled the spacecraft's powered landing.

  • Powered lunar descent
  • Landing legs and footpads
  • Descent propellant storage
  • Equipment and surface support

02. ASCENT STAGE

The upper section housed the astronauts and the systems needed to leave the Moon. After the surface mission, its ascent engine launched the astronauts back into lunar orbit.

  • Astronaut cabin
  • Ascent propulsion system
  • Navigation and control systems
  • Rendezvous with the Command Module

SCIENCE / DISCOVERIES

WHAT DID THE MISSION LEAVE US?

Apollo 11 was not only a historic human achievement. It also produced important scientific material and measurements.

01

LUNAR SAMPLES

The astronauts collected approximately 21.5 kilograms of lunar rocks and soil. These samples allowed scientists to investigate the Moon's geological history.

02

LASER REFLECTOR

A laser retroreflector array was placed on the lunar surface. Scientists can direct laser pulses towards it and measure the Earth-Moon distance with great precision.

03

ENGINEERING LEGACY

Apollo 11 demonstrated the effectiveness of complex spacecraft navigation, propulsion, communication and human-machine coordination.

THE ABANDONED ARTIFACT

WHY IS EAGLE STILL ON THE MOON?

After the astronauts completed their lunar activities, they returned to orbit using Eagle's ascent stage. The descent stage was not designed to return to Earth. It remained at the landing site.

THE DESCENT STAGE REMAINS

The lower half of Eagle stayed at Tranquility Base after the astronauts departed. It remains an artifact of the first human lunar landing.

The ascent stage, in contrast, was used to return the astronauts to lunar orbit. It was later discarded and eventually impacted the Moon.

The laser reflector and several other scientific and commemorative objects were also left on the lunar surface.

THE LEGACY CONTINUES

THE MISSION ENDED.
THE ACHIEVEMENT REMAINS.

Apollo 11 proved that humans could travel to another world, operate there and return safely. Eagle's descent stage remains on the Moon, while the knowledge gained from Apollo continues to influence lunar exploration and modern space engineering.
